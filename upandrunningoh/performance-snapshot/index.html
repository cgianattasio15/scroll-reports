The Fix · Middle of Funnel

Three moves to stop the leak

From the Scroll Content Engine · Idea → Format → Hook → Data
1

Prioritize save-first and share-first formats

Content designed for the bookmark and the send. Saves and shares are the strongest signals the algorithm rewards, and the clearest sign of a future customer.

+What this looks like for Up and Running
  • “The right shoe for how you run” series: stability vs neutral, cushion vs speed, matched to foot type. The save-before-your-fitting post.
  • “What to wear at every temperature” race-day guide: the classic running save, screenshotted every season.
  • “5 mistakes first-time 5K runners make”: a save and a send-to-a-friend in one post.
  • “Tag your running partner” prompts on event recaps: the share reflex your 5K content already earns.
2

Hold curiosity and attention all the way through

The first two seconds earn the view; the rest of the post has to keep it. Retention sits below target because attention leaks mid-post, not just at the open.

+What this looks like for Up and Running
  • Open with the payoff (the fit reveal, the tip), then hold with an open loop: “the third one surprises most runners.”
  • Pattern interrupts every few seconds: cuts, angle changes, props, on-screen text teasing the next beat.
  • Countdown and list structures that pay off at the end: “3 fits for fall, save the last one.”
  • One idea per post. A tight promise is what keeps attention through the close.
3

Stronger stories: real, behind the scenes, alive

Shift stories from polished graphics to authentic, in-real-life moments built to invite interaction. This is where the community actually talks back.

+What this looks like for Up and Running
  • Raw fitting moments and staff picks, shot on a phone, not designed graphics.
  • New-arrival unboxings and “the first thing you see walking in this week.”
  • Race-weekend takeovers from the team, live from the course.
  • Polls and this-or-that woven into real moments: “which colorway gets the wall display?”

The Fix · Bottom of Funnel

Make people want what you sell

Your link in bio and app are already in good shape · the job is making people want to tap
1

An athlete spotlight signature series

Follow up with every customer who got fitted and raced, then celebrate them on your page. Proof without a pitch: people buy to belong, and every spotlight recruits the next athlete.

+How the series works
  • The motion: fitting → they race → we follow up (“how did it go?”) → “can we spotlight you?” → feature post.
  • A named series (think “Finish Line” spotlights) so followers expect it and athletes want in.
  • Frictionless entry: “comment RACE and we’ll set up your spotlight” via DM automation.
  • Why it converts: recurring customer-feature series are among the highest-converting plays in local retail, and nobody else in the market is doing the race follow-up.
2

Monthly themes that mirror the store

One product focus per month, and what you see walking in is what you see on Instagram. The feed and the front-of-store display tell the same story.

+What this looks like for Up and Running
  • Shoes month, apparel month, recovery month: the in-store promotion becomes the content theme, framed as “this month we’re obsessed with,” never “sale.”
  • A fun in-store series: a kid (or a customer) picks any item in the store, and an expert explains what it actually does.
  • Corporate fittings stay on the map: the “On the Road” fit-day series keeps proof in front of the next business.
3

Expert point-of-view content, built on your ideal customer

Reverse-engineer who you serve: their challenges, obstacles, and dream outcomes, then publish the expertise that solves them. Authority is what makes buying feel obvious.

+What this looks like for Up and Running
  • Solution content mapped to real problems: shin splints, first-5K nerves, shoes that never quite fit, the PR chase.
  • Point-of-view commentary: “Why Brooks took off this year,” “what nobody tells you about carbon plates.” Takes only a real expert can make.
  • The journey stays easy: the content builds the want; your organized site and app catch the click.
